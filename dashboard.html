⚠️ Main Challenges with Home-Grown AI Tools

Based on engineering team responses, here are the key challenges preventing AI-native alternatives:

1. High Development Cost & Resource Requirements

  • Significant multi-year engineering effort required to match commercial feature sets
  • Need for dedicated cross-functional teams (engineers, QA, security, technical writers)
  • Substantial ongoing investment in hardware, tooling, and expertise
  • Development would divert resources from higher-value product initiatives
Example: "Building and maintaining an equivalent capability would require substantial ongoing investment in hardware, tooling, and expertise" - WorkloadWisdom

2. Specialized Domain Expertise Required

  • Commercial tools have decades of development and industry-standard features
  • Complex functionality difficult to replicate (e.g., installer technologies, container orchestration)
  • High risk of missing critical features that commercial tools provide
  • Need for deep knowledge in specialized areas (security protocols, storage testing, etc.)
Example: "InstallShield is a mature, industry-standard application packaging and installation platform with decades of development focused on Windows installer technologies" - InstallShield

3. Continuous Maintenance Burden

  • Need for continuous updates to support new OS releases, security requirements, and standards
  • Rapidly evolving ecosystems (Kubernetes, cloud-native technologies) require constant updates
  • Long-term maintenance commitment beyond initial development effort
  • Ongoing security patching and vulnerability management
Example: "Kubernetes and the surrounding cloud-native ecosystem evolve rapidly. Maintaining compatibility would require a dedicated platform engineering organization" - OpenShift

4. Performance & Reliability Concerns

  • Commercial tools have proven reliability and performance at scale
  • Home-grown solutions require extensive testing and iterations to ensure production quality
  • Risk of impacting development velocity during development and testing phases
  • Difficulty reproducing real-world conditions and edge cases
Example: "Performance of any solution is a key challenge that VectorCAST has proven reliable for. Using a home-grown alternative would require extensive iterations of development and test" - VectorCAST

5. Limited Strategic Value & ROI

  • Many tools are supporting infrastructure rather than core differentiating capabilities
  • Commercial solutions are cost-effective compared to internal development costs
  • Vendor support, regular updates, and proven reliability from commercial products
  • Internal development would duplicate capabilities already available off-the-shelf
Example: "It would take far too much time and resources, even with extensive use of AI, to perfect an alternative to a piece of already working off-the-shelf software which only costs a few hundred dollars per year" - Burp Suite

6. AI Cannot Replace Core Infrastructure

  • AI can assist with user experience, documentation, and troubleshooting
  • But AI does not replace underlying core functionality (installer runtime, container orchestration, etc.)
  • Complex infrastructure still needs to be engineered and maintained
  • AI is a productivity tool, not a replacement for specialized platforms
Example: "While AI could potentially improve user experience through package creation assistance, the underlying installer-authoring, packaging, deployment, testing, and lifecycle-management capabilities would still need to be engineered and maintained" - InstallShield

7. Cross-Platform & Ecosystem Complexity

  • Tools support multiple operating systems and environments (Windows, Linux, UNIX)
  • Developing cross-platform support requires substantial engineering effort
  • Integration with existing enterprise systems and compliance requirements
  • Need to maintain compatibility with industry standards and protocols
Example: "InstallAnywhere provides installer creation and deployment capabilities across multiple operating systems. Developing and maintaining this level of cross-platform support internally would require substantial engineering effort" - InstallAnywhere
